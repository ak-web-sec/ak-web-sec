What Is a Penetration Test?

A penetration test is a manual, simulated attack against your applications and infrastructure. Unlike automated vulnerability scans, penetration testing involves human expertise to identify business logic flaws, chained attack paths, and exploitable vulnerabilities that tools cannot detect. Every engagement follows an OWASP-aligned methodology and delivers a detailed report with evidence and remediation steps.

🔍

Web Application Testing

Authentication bypass, injection flaws, IDOR, XSS, CSRF, and business logic vulnerabilities in web apps and APIs.

📱

Mobile App Testing

Local storage analysis, certificate pinning bypass, reverse engineering, and API security for iOS and Android.

🌐

Network & Infrastructure

External reconnaissance, exposed services, misconfigurations, and segmentation testing.

📜

Clear Reporting

Executive summary, risk-rated findings with CVSS scores, evidence screenshots, and step-by-step remediation.

Methodology

1

Scoping

We define what's in scope, out of scope, testing windows, and emergency contacts. A formal rules of engagement document is signed before any testing begins.

2

Reconnaissance

Passive and active information gathering. We map your attack surface without touching production systems until authorized.

3

Testing

Manual testing aligned with OWASP Testing Guide v4.2. We test for injection, broken auth, sensitive data exposure, XXE, broken access control, and more.

4

Reporting & Retest

Detailed report delivered within 5 business days. We include a retest of all Critical and High findings at no extra charge.

Rules of Engagement

Before any penetration test begins, we agree on the following:

  • In scope: Specific domains, IPs, applications, and APIs to be tested.
  • Out of scope: Social engineering, physical access, and denial-of-service attacks are excluded unless explicitly agreed in writing.
  • Testing hours: Business hours (08:00–17:00 SAST) unless after-hours testing is requested.
  • Emergency contact: A direct line for critical findings that require immediate attention.
  • Authorization: Written proof of authorization is required before any testing begins.

The POPIA Compliance Checklist for Small Business Websites

POPIA has been law since 2021, yet most South African websites still treat compliance as a copy-paste privacy policy buried in the footer. That's not enough — and the Information Regulator is starting to enforce.

What POPIA Actually Requires

The Protection of Personal Information Act gives individuals rights over their data and places obligations on anyone who collects, processes, or stores personal information. For websites, this means more than a privacy policy.

The Developer's Checklist

1. Lawful Processing

Every form, cookie, and tracking pixel needs a lawful basis. For most websites, this will be consent or legitimate interest. If you're using consent, it must be:

  • Freely given — no pre-ticked boxes
  • Specific — separate consent for marketing vs analytics
  • Informed — tell users exactly what you're doing with their data
  • Documented — keep a record of when and how consent was obtained

2. Data Minimisation

Only collect what you actually need. If your contact form asks for a phone number but you never call anyone, remove the field. Every extra field is a compliance risk.

3. Security Measures

  • HTTPS everywhere — no exceptions
  • Encrypt personal data at rest if stored in databases
  • Limit access — not every employee needs to see every submission
  • Log access to personal data and review those logs

4. Retention & Deletion

You can't keep personal data forever. Set a retention policy: "We delete contact form submissions after 24 months" — and actually do it. POPIA gives individuals the right to deletion, and if you don't know where their data lives, you can't comply.

5. Third-Party Processors

If you use Formspree, Mailchimp, Google Analytics, or any other service that touches personal data, you need a data processing agreement with them. Most major providers have a standard DPA you can sign.

6. Cookie Consent

Essential cookies (login sessions, security) don't need consent. Everything else — analytics, marketing, social widgets — does. Your banner must let users opt out of non-essential cookies, not just inform them.

Penalties Are Real

POPIA violations can result in fines up to R10 million or 10 years imprisonment for serious offences. For most businesses, the reputational damage of a data breach will hurt more than the fine.

Compliance isn't a once-off project. It's an ongoing process. Start with this checklist, document everything, and review your posture at least annually.

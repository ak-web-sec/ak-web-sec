Why South African SMEs Are Prime Targets for Ransomware

Smaller teams, limited budgets, and outdated systems make SMEs attractive to attackers. Here's how to stay protected without breaking the bank.

South African SMEs often operate under the assumption that they're "too small to be targeted." The reality is the opposite: automated ransomware tools scan the entire internet indiscriminately. If your RDP port is open, your WordPress plugin is outdated, or your email lacks MFA, you're a target — regardless of company size.

What you can do today:

  • Enable multi-factor authentication on every business account
  • Keep WordPress/plugins updated or move to a managed host
  • Close unused ports (especially RDP on 3389)
  • Have offline backups that aren't connected to your network

The average ransomware demand against South African businesses in 2025 was R850,000. A R500/month monitoring plan looks cheap in comparison.

POPIA Compliance: A Practical Checklist for Websites

You don't need a lawyer to get started. Here's a developer-friendly checklist for making your website POPIA-compliant.

The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) isn't just a legal checkbox — it's a framework for handling user data responsibly. Most SA business websites collect at least a name and email. That makes you a "responsible party" under the act.

Quick checklist:

  • Publish a privacy policy that explains what you collect and why
  • Use SSL/TLS (HTTPS) on every page — not just checkout
  • Only collect data you actually need (data minimisation)
  • Get explicit consent before adding emails to newsletters
  • Have a process for users to request deletion of their data
  • Store passwords hashed (bcrypt/Argon2) — never plain text
  • Keep access logs for breach investigation purposes

Load Shedding & Your Website: Staying Online When the Grid Goes Down

How to choose hosting, caching, and monitoring strategies that survive South Africa's power challenges.

Eskom's grid instability isn't going away. If your web server sits in a building without backup power, your site goes down every time the lights go out — and your customers notice.

Strategies that work:

  • Cloud hosting: Move to AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud with multi-region failover
  • CDN: Cloudflare or Fastly can serve cached pages even if your origin is down
  • Static sites: JAMstack (Next.js, Hugo) means no server to go down
  • Monitoring: Know you're down before your customers tell you

OWASP Top 10 2026: What Changed and What Matters

A breakdown of the latest OWASP updates and what they mean for your web applications.

The OWASP Top 10 was refreshed in late 2025, and two major shifts affect South African businesses directly: the rise of AI-generated insecure code, and the formal inclusion of insecure design as a category.

Key changes:

  • Insecure Design: Security can't be bolted on later. It needs to be in your architecture from day one.
  • Software and Data Integrity Failures: Supply chain attacks (think SolarWinds) mean you need to verify every dependency.
  • Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF): Cloud metadata endpoints make this more dangerous than ever.

If your last security review was before 2025, your risk profile has changed. Our quarterly pentests are aligned to the latest OWASP framework so you're always testing against current threats.

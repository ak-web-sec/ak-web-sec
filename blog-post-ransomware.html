Why South African SMEs Are the New Ransomware Target

If you run a small business in South Africa, you might assume ransomware gangs only chase big corporates with deep pockets. That assumption is costing local SMEs millions.

The Numbers Don't Lie

In 2025, South Africa ranked among the top 10 most targeted countries for ransomware globally. Small and medium enterprises made up over 60% of reported victims — not because they hold the most data, but because they're the easiest targets.

Large enterprises have dedicated security teams, 24/7 monitoring, and incident response retainers. Most SMEs don't even have a firewall configured correctly. Attackers know this. They use automated scanners to find vulnerable systems, and when they hit a small business, the ransom demand is calibrated to what the victim can afford — usually R50,000 to R500,000.

How They Get In

The most common entry points we see during penetration tests:

  • Outdated software — WordPress plugins, old Windows servers, unpatched VPNs
  • Weak or reused passwords — especially on RDP and admin panels exposed to the internet
  • Phishing emails — increasingly sophisticated, often impersonating SARS, banks, or suppliers
  • No backups — or backups connected to the same network, which get encrypted too

Why Paying Doesn't Work

Even if you pay, there's no guarantee you'll get your data back. Worse, paying marks you as a "payer" — your details get shared among criminal networks, and you'll likely be hit again within 12 months.

What You Can Do Today

  • Enable multi-factor authentication on every admin account
  • Keep software updated — especially content management systems and plugins
  • Segment your network so one compromised machine can't reach everything
  • Test your backups — restore a file monthly to make sure they actually work
  • Run a security assessment at least once a year

Ransomware isn't a big-business problem anymore. It's a South African business problem. The good news: most attacks are preventable with basic hygiene and continuous monitoring.

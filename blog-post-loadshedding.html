Load Shedding and Website Resilience: What South African Businesses Should Know

Load shedding is a security problem, not just an inconvenience. Availability is one of the three pillars of information security, and in South Africa it is the pillar most under attack — not by hackers, but by the grid.

What Actually Goes Down

When your area is shed, several things can fail at once, and most business owners only discover the dependency chain after the fact:

  • Your website — if it is hosted on a local server, an office NAS, or a budget provider without generator backup.
  • Your DNS — if your domain is served by a single local nameserver, your site becomes unreachable even when the web server is fine.
  • Your email — self-hosted mail servers bounce messages during outages; senders may not retry indefinitely.
  • Your fibre and router — the ONT and router in your office are on municipal power unless you have a UPS.
  • Card payments and VoIP — both die with the internet connection.

Build for the Grid You Have

1. Get off local hardware

Static sites hosted on a CDN-backed platform have no server to lose power. For dynamic applications, use a hosting provider with documented generator and battery backup, or a cloud region outside South Africa with a local CDN edge.

2. Put a CDN in front of everything

A CDN such as Cloudflare caches your pages at the edge. If your origin goes dark, cached pages can still be served, and features like Always Online keep a fallback copy available. It also absorbs the traffic spikes that follow an outage.

3. Use redundant DNS

Use a global anycast DNS provider with multiple nameservers on separate networks. DNS is the cheapest single point of failure to fix, and the most damaging to leave broken.

4. Move email to a managed provider

Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 removes email from your load shedding risk entirely. If you must self-host, configure a backup MX so mail queues instead of bouncing.

5. Protect the last mile

A small UPS or inverter for your ONT, router and switch keeps the office online through a 2-hour slot. Add an LTE failover on your router so a fibre node outage does not stop work.

6. Monitor from outside

Internal monitoring is useless during an outage — it is off too. External uptime checks from multiple regions tell you within minutes that customers cannot reach you, which is exactly what our monitoring plans do at five-minute intervals.

The Security Angle Nobody Mentions

Outages create security risk in ways that are easy to miss:

  • Missed patches and scans — scheduled updates that fail during an outage often never run again.
  • Failed backups — a backup job interrupted mid-run may leave an unusable archive. Test restores, do not trust green ticks.
  • Log gaps — if your logging host was down, you lose the evidence you would need after an incident.
  • Phishing cover — attackers use “system offline, verify your details” pretexts precisely because outages are normal here.
  • Hardware wear — repeated hard power cycles kill drives and corrupt databases.

A Realistic Baseline

For most South African SMEs, resilience does not need to be expensive. A static or CDN-fronted site, anycast DNS, hosted email, a UPS for network gear, LTE failover, and external uptime monitoring will carry you through Stage 6 for a few hundred rand a month.
